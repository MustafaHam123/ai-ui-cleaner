# Anti-generic rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2. A substantial page should reach at least 12/16 with no zero. Fix the lowest dimensions first.

## 1. Content specificity

- **0:** Placeholder or interchangeable marketing language.
- **1:** Mostly specific, with generic headings or proof.
- **2:** Hierarchy and copy clearly belong to this product and audience.

## 2. Compositional idea

- **0:** Centered hero followed by repeated equal cards and alternating feature rows.
- **1:** Some variation, but no memorable organizing principle.
- **2:** One clear idea controls pacing, scale, grouping, and transitions.

## 3. Information hierarchy

- **0:** Everything has similar weight or depends on containers for separation.
- **1:** Main actions are visible but secondary information competes.
- **2:** Importance and sequence are understandable before reading details.

## 4. Typography

- **0:** Default sizing, arbitrary weights, or oversized gradient headlines as decoration.
- **1:** Consistent scale with limited expressive purpose.
- **2:** Type supports the product's character, density, and reading behavior.

## 5. Spatial rhythm

- **0:** Identical padding and section spacing everywhere.
- **1:** Mostly consistent with occasional purposeful changes.
- **2:** Repetition and contrast create deliberate pacing without losing alignment.

## 6. Interaction and states

- **0:** Static happy path or motion added without meaning.
- **1:** Core states exist but feedback is generic.
- **2:** States, transitions, and feedback explain what changed and what to do next.

## 7. Originality without novelty theater

- **0:** Trend collage: glass cards, glow, blobs, excessive pills, or gratuitous 3D.
- **1:** Competent but visually interchangeable.
- **2:** Distinctive choices follow from the content, audience, and product behavior.

## 8. Responsive integrity

- **0:** Desktop compressed into one column with overflow or lost priority.
- **1:** Usable at common sizes with a few awkward transitions.
- **2:** Mobile reprioritizes content and controls while preserving the design thesis.

Common signals to challenge rather than ban automatically:

- A vague headline above two generic calls to action.
- Three or four equal feature cards used regardless of content.
- Rounded containers around every group.
- Gradient text, purple glow, or blurred blobs used as instant personality.
- Logos and testimonials added without meaningful evidence.
- Excessive centered alignment.
- Icons carrying meaning without labels.
- One animation preset applied to every section.
